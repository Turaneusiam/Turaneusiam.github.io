---
title: Childhood Memory
category: Recollections
---
When I was a child, there was a stretch of meadow near my home.
Through the turning of seasons, it blossomed with wild clover, dandelions, foxtails, snakeberries, asters, and ground ivy,
an intimate, untamed world breathing beneath the clouds.

In spring, I remember a pond hidden behind the hill.
A winding wooden bridge led to a little pavilion at its heart,
where passersby sometimes paused to rest.
I found tadpoles there,
kept them in large glass jars,
and watched as they grew their tiny legs,
the silent miracle of transformation unfolding in my hands.

By the pond stood a great tree with a wasp’s nest in its branches.
On still summer nights, the chorus of cicadas rose and fell,
the loudest voice of the landscape.
The basketball court nearby was always empty;
I never saw anyone play there.
I would cling to the hoop,
my feet balanced on the bolts and screws that held it to the ground.
Not far away stood two rows of tall plane trees,
solemn and proud.

When autumn rain fell,
their leaves blanketed the path in golden brown,
and footsteps carved a road through them.
The air was thick with the scent of earth and its **muddy fragile underbelly**, 
a smell that deepened in late harvest
and slowly faded as winter drew near.

Winter was the season of imaginations. 
At the end of the sycamore path stood a half-open wooden door,
not the main entrance; I never saw that one.
The adults said it was on the other side of the wall,
outside the school grounds.
But within those walls,
was **the entirety of my childhood**.

With a small shovel, I scraped away winter bit by bit,
sending snow tumbling from my balcony
onto the one below,
where a new birth was waiting to form.

Then spring would quietly return,
softly brushing the earth awake.
And I would find myself a step closer to adolescence,
to adulthood, 
a step closer to leaving home.
The seasons of last year had barely been savored
**before my shoes grew tight,
my hair grew longer,
my shadow stretched further,
and my clothes grew thicker,
as did my thoughts**.

When night fell,
I ran beneath dim starlight,
chasing my lengthened shadow.
Behind every closed curtain,
I imagined silhouettes of emotions,
some dining, some watching TV,
some simply existing,
hustling, full, alive and whole.

小时候，我家附近是一片草地，随着四季交迭变更，草地上长满苜蓿、蒲公英、狗尾巴草、蛇莓、马兰、地锦草等野花野草。春季，我想起了小山后的一片鱼塘，有蜿蜒曲折的小桥通往池塘中心的小亭，偶尔会有人在亭子里驻足、歇脚。池塘里可以捉蝌蚪，捉来放在玻璃瓶里养，就可以观察它们长出两条后腿的过程。鱼塘边有一棵大树，树上有一个马蜂窝。静谧的夏夜，此起彼伏的蝉鸣是这里最响亮的声音。篮球场总是空旷的，记忆中从没有人来这里打篮球。我会抱住篮球架，脚踩着用来固定篮球架的螺钉和螺母。这里不远处，矗立着两排高大笔挺的梧桐树。每逢秋雨浇打，落叶就厚厚地铺在地上，被人踏出一条金色和深棕色的路。那时的雨混杂着泥土的腥气。这种气味在深秋时最浓，之后会逐渐消散。当它消失殆尽时，冬季已差不多来临。冬季是遐想的季节。在梧桐小道的尽头，我看见了虚掩的小门。那不是面包房的正门，我从来没有见过面包房的正门。听大人说，正门在围墙的另一面，在学校外面。而围墙里面，已是我全部的童年。我拿着小铲子，把冬天一点一点铲走，雪从我的阳台上坠落，落在楼下那个新生命的阳台上。 来年的春天悄悄接近大地，我离成长和背井离乡又更近了一步。去年的四季还没有来得及好好品味，鞋子就变短了。头发变长了，影子变长了，衣服也更绵密、厚重了。还有思绪。 夜幕降临，我踩着拉长的影子，在黯淡的星光下奔跑。家家户户闭合的窗帘里仿佛有人物的轮廓，有的在吃饭，有的在看电视，有的不知在做什么，忙碌而充实。
