---
title: Calcium Dreams
category: People
---

She had calcium dreams last night. These are dreams in which imagination and reality ossify, turning pale and brittle, crumbling into dust at the faintest touch of morning light. It began with recollections of a school standing in the idyllic, serene expanse of the prairies, a soft fragment of youthhood, its walls heavy with time, its floors whispering the secrets of forgotten footsteps, its hallways stretched on like fading memories, and a rusted gate guarded the rooftop.

The rooftop was a symbolic place in all those commonplace, youthful tales, familiar plots, familiar faces. They would gaze into the sky, that boundless void so immense and so close, and without hesitation, it embraced them. All of a sudden, they felt a strong impulse to dance, reliving the rituals of the ancient ones who once stood tall upon this relentless land. They danced to break free from the city, a mirror of reality filled to its brim. They danced until the night devoured their rhythm, and the stars were consumed by the invisible force of existence.

She sat down in the lecture hall, seeing the rows of wooden desks, the hum of fluorescent lights, hearing a voice static and circular, droning on and on. 

“This,” it said, “is what you’ll be tested on.”

“Tell me, students, what is the meaning of chivalry?”

Chivalry, she thought, it must be a horse. Yes, it has to do with horses, doesn’t it? For some strange reasons she could not explain, her memory was vague, her reasoning slow to surface.
She saw a knight in shining armor, a gleaming sword in hand, running far and wide. And in her innocent, sprouting heart, she felt the strange and tender stirring of something fierce. She knew then, she would have to be her own sword and shield, the one who rides into the wind.

The bell rang, and the mechanic voice ceased. Startled, like wild horses breaking from their pasture, the children ran, flooding the spiral staircases with laughter, echoes, and the pulse of freedom. They dispersed in all directions, into the compassionate prairie, where words no longer needed grammar, where sentences flowed backward, where creativity was untamed, and where happiness and sorrow wore colors that no tongue could name.
